OverviewThe cost of a job was decided on site and discovered weeks later

Etasuisse AG is a Swiss engineering company based in Bilten, working across geothermal energy, plastic piping systems and technical water treatment. Much of its work happens on site: teams install systems, fill and treat large heating and cooling installations, and deliver mobile heating. Every project involves people, hours, materials and vehicles that all need to be tracked and costed.

All of that lived in Excel. Hours, materials, vehicle use and expenses were recorded by hand and combined later, which made it slow to see what a project was really costing and whether it was making money.

Etasuisse PM replaces those spreadsheets with two connected parts: an admin web dashboard for managing projects, reviewing costs and producing financial reports, and an operator mobile app for field teams to record work as it happens. Both read and write the same project record.

01 ResearchFollowing the job before designing the screen

I started with five questions and came back to them whenever the discussion drifted toward features.

  1. What happens between a project being agreed and an invoice being sent?
  2. Where is cost created, and who is there when it happens?
  3. Which numbers are retyped, and where do they go wrong?
  4. What does the office need to see to decide whether a project is healthy?
  5. What conditions do operators work in when they record their work?

02 Market AnalysisWhy a small field team needed a real system

Etasuisse’s work sits inside a market that is large but changeable. Heat pump sales in Switzerland peaked at around 43,000 units in 2023 and fell to around 30,000 in 2024 and 2025; ground-source systems made up 27% of sales in 2025, and the national target is 1.5 million heat pumps by 2050. When demand swings, knowing which projects actually make money matters more.

Panko, Spreadsheet Errors: What We Know

Two findings shaped the brief. Field technicians spend about 18% of their working hours on administration, more than 7 hours a week, and 81% work overtime at least monthly to keep up. At Swiss labour costs of around CHF 63.62 an hour, that is expensive time. And spreadsheets are fragile: in field audits since 1997, about 9 in 10 spreadsheets contained errors. Retyping site notes into a workbook combines both problems.

04 Product RequirementsFrom problem to product

Problem statementEtasuisse needs a way for field teams to record hours, materials, travel and expenses as they work, and for the office to see each project’s true cost and profit without retyping spreadsheets.

How might we

Three product concepts

Concept A kept the retyping and the fragile formulas. Concept B tracked tasks, but not the hours, materials and rates that decide profit. Concept C was the only one that captured data where it was created and costed it in one place.

Functional requirements

Non-functional requirements

  1. Swiss defaults: CHF currency, 24-hour time and a language setting, with English as the default.
  2. Usable on site: large tap targets, simple sections and units built into each field.
  3. Trustworthy numbers: rates apply from a date, so changing a rate never rewrites past projects.
  4. Safe access: roles decide who can edit, who can only view, and who can log work.

05 User FlowsTwo products, one map each

Each product has its own navigation, built around the job its users came to do. The operator app is three tabs deep at most; the admin dashboard is organised around projects, with money inside every project.

11 Developer HandoffDesigning the numbers, not just the screens

The hardest part to hand over was not a layout but the arithmetic behind it. I documented where every cost line comes from, so developers and the office would agree on what each number means.

Cost lineBuilt fromExample from the prototype
LabourHours worked × labour rate7.5 h × CHF 25.00 = CHF 187.50
VehicleDistance × km rate, higher with a trailerCHF 1.00/km, or CHF 1.50/km with trailer
MaterialsQuantity × material rateNexion at CHF 3.67 per litre
ExpensesEntered per project with title and amountHotel, 2-night stay
ProfitBilled to customer minus partner billing, fees and costsShown on every project tab
  1. Rates are versioned with a valid-from date; logs use the rate in force on the day of work.
  2. Status and billing are separate fields, so a completed project can still be unbilled.
  3. Every log belongs to one operator and one project, which keeps edits accountable.
  4. Designed states for empty lists, saving, success and errors on both products.

OutcomesWhat the design delivers

Etasuisse PM is designed and prototyped end to end, for both the office and the field. It replaces retyped spreadsheets with one project record: operators capture work as it happens, rates turn it into cost automatically, and every project shows what was billed, what it cost and what it made.

ReflectionWhat this project taught me

ChallengeLesson
Two very different usersDesign each product for its context, then join them through one shared record.
Money as the real productStart from the numbers the business decides on, then design the screens that produce them.
Rates that changeTreat time as part of the data model, or history quietly changes.
Work done with gloves onField forms need sections, units and defaults more than features.

If I started again, I would sit with operators on site in the first week and time how long their current notes take. That baseline would make the value of the app easy to prove.
